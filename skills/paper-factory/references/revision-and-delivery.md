# Revision and delivery

## Shorten with an explicit budget

When shortening to meet a submission limit, verify the target venue/year's current rules from the official guide: main text, references, appendices, supplementary material, template, font, and anonymous-submission requirements. If a rule remains ambiguous, state the narrow uncertainty and proceed with unaffected edits. Do not claim compliance from an assumption about appendices. For a requested percentage cut to an excerpt, use a consistent stated word-count basis; no venue lookup or full build is needed unless requested.

Measure the current compiled artifact and section lengths. Preserve explicit user choices about figures and appendices. Save space first by removing duplicated motivation, related-work cataloging, section previews, repeated scope defenses, and development history. Merge paragraphs with the same argumentative job. Preserve results, units, denominators, evidence needed to evaluate them, and methodological information needed to interpret the study.

Moving a figure to an appendix requires checking its references, caption, placement, and whether the main argument still stands. Do not change a figure the user reserved for their own edit. Estimate potential savings before editing if useful; measure actual savings after compilation. Word-count reduction does not guarantee a page-count reduction. Avoid shrinking type or spacing outside venue rules.

Check semantic preservation separately from counting. A modifier restricting a sentence to a particular population or topic can be more important than a repeated number. Compare the old and new proposition, including its conditions and attribution, rather than relying on unchanged numerical tokens, citation keys, or a smooth reading. Keep necessary qualifiers even when they reduce the achieved cut slightly.

## Revise across dependent sections

For each substantive change, inspect its dependents:

| Change | Common downstream checks |
|---|---|
| Research question or contribution | Title, abstract, introduction, discussion, conclusion |
| Corpus or sampling boundary | Methods, counts, table captions, generalizations, supplement |
| Evidence interpretation | Findings, summary tables, abstract, limitations, generated views |
| Method or scoring correction | Protocol description, results, comparison, reproducibility statement |
| Figure/table placement | Callouts, numbering, captions, appendix references, page count |
| Bibliography merge | Used keys, duplicate identities, version conflicts, compiler warnings |

Do not rewrite every section for a local change. Follow actual dependencies. Start from the latest authoritative version; preserve deliberate user deletions and coauthor decisions.

## Coauthor and reviewer responses

Use an existing response document or [the revision ledger](../assets/revision-ledger.csv) for multi-comment work. Record the comment, actionable request, decision, changed location, supporting evidence, and status. Distinguish `addressed`, `partially-addressed`, `disagree-with-reason`, and `pending`.

Answer the concern before describing edits. Point to exact changes and explain their effect. When declining a suggestion, use the evidence or study scope rather than defensive tone. Do not manufacture agreement, reviewer identities, approval, or new analyses. If comments conflict, implement the compatible parts and identify the material decision that remains for the authors.

A response can say an analysis was added only after its result exists and has been checked. If new analysis is authorized and needed, route it through the relevant analysis workflow, preserving protocols and evidence; writing alone is not experimental validation.

## Bibliography integrity

Preserve keys already used by the manuscript when merging libraries. Compare bibliographic identity before deduplicating; title, year, DOI, preprint version, and publication venue can differ meaningfully. Resolve case-only key conflicts deliberately. Keep a recoverable original for substantial merges. Compile/check all citations after the merge; do not silently replace cited versions with newer ones.

## Delivery checks

Use checks proportionate to the change, and inspect the actual deliverable:

- Source claims and numbers agree across the modified sections and the evidence record.
- Every citation resolves to the intended work; quotations and page locators were checked.
- Cross-references, figure/table numbering, labels, and appendix callouts resolve.
- The rendered pages have readable figures/tables, no cut-off content, broken equations, or unexpected blank pages.
- Final page count and required declarations reflect the verified rules when submission compliance is in scope.
- Anonymization and supplement consistency are checked when relevant to the requested submission version.
- The delivered source and rendered file correspond to the same revision.

For a focused copyedit, do not create an elaborate test suite. For a substantial manuscript build, successful compilation and a rendered inspection are meaningful checks. Keep scientific, editorial, technical, and submission checks distinct in the completion report.

If a changed source has an owning generator, update it or record the remaining regeneration risk. Preserve historical model outputs and frozen reports; do not overwrite evidence to make the manuscript appear internally consistent.
