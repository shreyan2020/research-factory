# Design basis

This workflow combines direct presentation-revision requests with the supplied Presenting Your Research (PYR) materials. These are design inputs, not evidence that this skill improves learning outcomes. Course files and private conversation records are not redistributed here.

## User decisions represented

The presentation revision history establishes preferences for coherent examples, realistic app screens, purposeful humor and emojis, question-first click reveals, minimal audience-facing meta-text, and edits based on the latest saved deck. Later classroom feedback also shows why pale card fills need projection checks. Individual requests to remove a particular picture or change a palette do not establish universal bans on pictures or colors.

## Apply judgment to the sources

Do not copy a handout's disclaimer footer into a presentation template. Keep necessary attribution and material evidence limitations, while moving production commentary into notes. Course example charts are illustrative assets, not datasets for new scientific claims.

Do not promote the audience handout's 55/38/7 percentages into a general law of presentation comprehension; no supporting primary evidence was established in this review. Treat chart suggestions as options: not every scatter plot needs a fitted line, not every quantitative result belongs in a bar chart, and not every qualitative theme is geographic. The actual question and data govern the choice.
