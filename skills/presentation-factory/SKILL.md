# Presentation factory

Turn the user's material into a presentation an audience can understand and a presenter can use. Own the work from source selection through the actual saved deliverable. This skill adds research and teaching judgment to the available presentation tools; it does not replace their format-specific instructions.

## Start from the intended use

Recover the audience, purpose, allotted speaking or teaching time, latest source version, requested destination, template, and any required slide count from the conversation and artifacts. Ask only for missing decisions that change the deliverable. Make reasonable choices for reversible design details and proceed.

Keep the current user-edited deck as the starting point. Preserve deliberate deletions, chosen terminology, and the scope of a requested revision. A fresh narrative does not authorize replacing a supplied design or rebuilding unrelated slides.

Read the available **Presentations** format skill before creating or changing a deck. Use its current routing and implementation guidance, and load a native Google Slides workflow when that is the destination. Apply **concise-artifacts** when available. If a companion skill is unavailable, preserve the user's chosen format, use supported tools, and report a concrete capability gap rather than pretending it was loaded. Do not copy versioned runtime paths into new work.

If the user requests only an outline, slide text, or speaker notes, deliver and check that text. Do not create a deck, require a rendering workflow, or report an export as pending when no exported deck was requested.

## Build through observable checkpoints

1. **Evidence and scope:** Identify the authoritative material, missing evidence, and the intended artifact. For a research deck, trace substantive claims and numbers to passages, tables, or saved experiment results. Do not silently convert proposals, synthetic examples, or automated labels into findings.
2. **Sequence:** Draft a compact slide map with each slide's purpose, visible content, supporting source, and any interaction or speaker note. It can stay in working notes; do not create a separate plan artifact for a small edit. Keep the requested total slide count, including the cover unless instructed otherwise.
3. **Representative slide:** When the visual system or tooling is new, build and render a content-heavy slide early. Use it to settle legibility, evidence density, and editability before expanding the deck. A known template or minor revision does not require a prototype.
4. **Production:** Build a coherent sequence in manageable batches. Keep claims and evidence together. Put facilitator reasoning, detailed timing, and backup answers in notes or a requested facilitator guide. Use visual evidence when it clarifies the point; do not fill space with decorations or repeated panels.
5. **Verification:** Inspect the actual saved artifact, all slide renders, and the material interactions required for delivery. Correct the cause of overflow or confusion before reducing type size. Apply the format skill's required checks; use [Verification and handoff](references/verification-and-handoff.md) to distinguish what each check establishes.
6. **Handoff:** Deliver the verified deck in the chosen destination and any requested companion materials. Mention only limitations that affect use. A saved Spaces presentation, a downloaded PPTX, and a live embedded slideshow are distinct outcomes.

## Use parallel work selectively

For a substantial deck, independent source checking, activity design, and an audience review can run in parallel after the brief and slide map are stable. Give each contributor a bounded section, source set, and output contract. Keep a single owner for the deck's structure and visual system. Avoid concurrent edits to the same slides or contradictory section narratives.

An independent reviewer should inspect the deck and source evidence, not merely approve the author's checklist. Ask them to identify the most consequential misunderstanding, unsupported claim, or delivery failure. For a small revision, a direct inspection is enough.

## Completion and learning

Finish when the requested content is present and evidence is traceable. For a deck deliverable, also check that the actual saved deck is readable and the required delivery path is verified or its remaining limitation is stated accurately. For text-only work, review the sequence and notes without implying artifact or live-delivery checks occurred. Do not keep polishing after those conditions are met without a reason.

If a material failure occurs, record its symptom, cause when known, attempted remedy, observed outcome, and the narrowly applicable lesson in the project's existing record. Do not promote a one-off tool failure into a permanent ban or change global skills without authorization.
