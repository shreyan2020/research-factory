# Literature-grounded motivation and interpretation

Read for substantive narrative creation or revision. The aim is a defensible relationship between the existing literature and our findings, not an exhaustive review by default. Reuse the project's bibliography, source ledger, and prior searches; refresh the parts that govern current positioning.

## 1. Establish the relevant research landscape

Search by the actual phenomenon, research question, proposed mechanism, task, and outcome, including equivalent terminology from adjacent fields when relevant. Start with user-nominated papers and the closest studies, then follow their references and citing work. Use reviews to orient the search and primary papers to substantiate consequential scientific claims.

Combine foundational explanations with recent, directly relevant evidence. Check work since the last documented search, or state the date range used when starting from scratch. Record search date, sources searched, key queries, and material access limits. Do not claim a systematic or exhaustive review without the corresponding method. Do not add a fixed quota of recent papers or replace a stronger older study with a weaker newer one merely for freshness.

Read the actual methods, results, and limitations needed for the comparison. Abstracts and snippets can identify candidates, but support only claims available in that inspected material. Verify bibliographic identity, version, publication status, and relevant corrections or retractions when encountered or consequential. Do not describe a preprint as a peer-reviewed publication or attribute a later revision's result to an older version. Preserve original bibliography keys when possible.

## 2. Construct the motivation as a supported argument

Use this progression where it fits the study:

**Important phenomenon or decision → established knowledge → specific unresolved tension or limitation → why resolving it matters → question and design → answer supported by our findings.**

For each transition, ask which source establishes the premise and whether the inference follows. For each central precedent, state the overlap before the difference. Identify the question, mechanism, material, population, measurement, or setting we share, then the exact extension or boundary our design can examine.

A meaningful gap can be an unresolved disagreement, uncertain mechanism, untested boundary, measurement mismatch, weakly supported generalization, or insufficient replication. An untried dataset/method combination alone does not establish scientific importance. “Not reported in the inspected paper,” “not accessible,” and “not found in this search” do not establish that no research exists.

After results, narrow the promised contribution to what was learned. If the study does not resolve the original gap, say what it does establish and what remains open. Do not rewrite the original hypothesis to predict the outcome. Keep later literature useful for current interpretation separate from evidence that historically motivated the study.

## 3. Contextualize each central finding

Maintain a compact comparison, not a procession of paper summaries:

| Our finding and evidence ID | Closest published finding/theory and exact locator | Shared construct, task, or mechanism | Material differences | Relationship and warranted interpretation | Remaining rival explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Use these relationships deliberately:

- **Corroborates:** the evidence is consistent with an existing account within comparable scope. Do not call it an independent replication unless design and independence justify that label.
- **Extends:** specify the additional population, condition, mechanism, scale, or observation actually examined, and why it is informative.
- **Qualifies:** identify a boundary or limitation on a prior inference, and the evidence exposing it.
- **Contradicts:** establish that the claims genuinely concern comparable quantities or propositions. Different tasks, evidence windows, measures, or a significant versus nonsignificant result do not alone demonstrate disagreement.
- **Not directly comparable:** explain the mismatch and retain the open question. Resemblance can motivate a hypothesis without validating the same mechanism.

One finding can relate differently to several papers. Include the strongest conflicting evidence and competing theoretical explanation. Literature can supply an interpretation; it cannot supply a measurement missing from our study. An external mechanism finding does not prove that mechanism operated in our sample.

## 4. Make the literature visible in the tree and paper

Add prior-work nodes for the premises needed to motivate the question and interpret the findings. Label each node as prior evidence, prior theory, our observation, or our interpretation. Link resemblance, extension, qualification, or challenge at the relevant claim rather than attaching all references to a generic background node.

The introduction establishes the importance, existing knowledge, and bounded question. Related work explains concrete overlap and differences. Results report our observations. Discussion connects those observations back to prior evidence and theory, considers alternatives, and updates the answer. Adapt this order to the paper type and venue without changing the distinctions.

Useful prose scaffolds, to be filled with verified content:

- “Prior studies establish [X] in [conditions]. Whether [Y] holds under [meaningful change] remains unresolved because [source-backed limitation].”
- “Our finding [F] resembles [specific result] in [respect], while differing in [design or observation]. This supports [bounded interpretation], although [rival] remains possible.”
- “The present design does not resolve [original uncertainty]. It establishes [narrower result], which changes [specific assumption or next question].”

These are reasoning prompts, not phrases to repeat mechanically. Citations must support the adjacent proposition, including statements about what prior work did or did not establish.

## Completion boundary

The literature grounding is ready for handoff when the central motivation premises have inspected sources, the closest relevant precedents have explicit overlap/difference comparisons, each central finding has a contextual interpretation, and the contribution remains defensible against the strongest identified precedent and counterevidence. Record unresolved access or novelty questions. Do not label the narrative current, novel, or publication-ready merely because its citation count increased.
