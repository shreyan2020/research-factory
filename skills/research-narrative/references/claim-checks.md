# Checks before selecting a narrative

Use the checks that bear on the central claims. Keep the working record compact; these are inferential decisions, not extra sections every paper must contain.

## Resolve the source before resolving the story

Identify the artifact and version being interpreted. An accepted poster, a full manuscript, a thesis adaptation, and a later reanalysis can share a title or dataset without sharing the same claims. Acceptance establishes publication status, not the correctness of every sentence.

Cross-check consequential claims across the abstract, original RQ/hypothesis wording, methods, detailed results, tables/captions including claim-changing appendix results, and conclusion. If a hypothesis label changes proposition, an effect changes sign, or a summary disagrees with the model, record both locations and the precise conflict. Do not quietly choose whichever supports the preferred story. Use the owning protocol or result record when available; otherwise narrow the framing and leave the disputed branch unresolved. A later corrected result may govern current interpretation without rewriting what the accepted version said.

Also check outcomes promised by contribution statements even when they fall outside numbered hypotheses. Give missing or out-of-scope evidence an explicit disposition; this does not require reporting every collected variable as a central finding.

## Connect constructs to what was actually observed

For a central claim, identify the target construct, its measure, assigned versus measured variables, measurement order, comparison, analysis unit, and conditioning or exclusions. Include the smallest explicit bridge that exposes a consequential mismatch.

Check scale direction before interpreting a sign: a higher score may mean greater concern, stronger protection, or more favorable evaluation. Preserve unresolved coding and inconsistent estimate/interval statements instead of inventing a corrected value.

- A reported belief is a measurement of belief, not direct access to a latent state. A perceived fairness rating is not an audit of allocation fairness.
- A measured participant characteristic is not randomized by random assignment of an interface. A subgroup association does not establish that adapting the interface to that characteristic will help.
- A post-outcome belief cannot be presented as an experimentally established cause of that outcome. Observational mediation needs identification assumptions beyond a fitted indirect path.
- A package that jointly varies length, tone, and scaffolding does not identify which component produced a difference. Task names such as “personalized” or “evaluative” are not evidence of the mechanism.
- Conditional analyses change the question: among movers, successful cases, or residual errors is different from the whole sample. A distance score that includes zero for correct answers does not isolate the severity of remaining errors.

Compare metrics by what they measure, not their shared label. Similar measures may support different claims; different measures may provide complementary evidence without being interchangeable.

Reconcile reported totals across filters, analysis units, and model-candidate sets before carrying counts into a headline. Keep unresolved arithmetic or denominator conflicts visible rather than silently selecting a number.

## Preserve the analysis decision rule

When primary and sensitivity analyses disagree, retain their actual hierarchy and uncertainty. Check the contrast, adjustment, tail, margin, and scored population before translating a result into “supported.” Do not choose the favorable test retrospectively or infer an unverified prespecified rule from the manuscript's conclusion. Separate no detected difference, demonstrated equivalence, and insufficient precision.

With interactions, distinguish a reference-group coefficient from a simple contrast or an aggregate effect. Support for an overall difference is not support in every subgroup; a significant result in one subgroup and a nonsignificant result in another does not itself establish a difference between them.

## Match the contribution to its evidence

For a method or system paper, link **prior theory or problem → design requirement → implemented component → evaluated property → remaining limitation**. Implementation, feasibility, comparative advantage, and benefit to users are distinct claims. A theoretical reason to want an explanation property does not show the system delivers it; a fidelity proxy does not demonstrate user understanding. Keep calibration or measurement contributions visible even when a performance gain is absent.

For multi-study papers, state the job of each study and the link between them: generation, evaluation, extension, reanalysis, or independent confirmation. Track shared participants, stimuli, annotations, and selection. Independently recruited evaluators can still assess artifacts selected from an earlier study; the narrative must preserve that dependency.

For a reanalysis, identify which earlier claim it changes and which it does not address. A revised explanation of one outcome does not automatically overturn every result from the original study.

For design implications, state the observed finding, the proposed action, and the extra assumption connecting them. Label an untested intervention as a proposal rather than an established remedy.

## Keep the reader's route focused

Full coverage belongs in the working register. The reader's main route needs the central uncertainty, decisive evidence, and qualifications that change its answer. Put secondary analyses in supporting branches with an explicit destination; do not turn every hypothesis into an equally prominent headline. Preserve the paper's positive explanatory or methodological contribution alongside its limits.
