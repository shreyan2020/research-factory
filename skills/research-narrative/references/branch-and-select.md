# Develop branches, then let the author select

Use this workflow for a new substantive narrative or a request to explore alternative framings. A narrative branch is an alternative organizing argument for the paper, not a subclaim within one argument. All branches share the same evidence and research history.

## Prepare a reviewable set

Usually develop two or three defensible branches; use the user's requested number when feasible. Distinguish them by the uncertainty they address, contribution, theoretical interpretation, or reader route. Different titles for the same argument are not different branches. Method, phenomenon, measurement, or boundary-condition framings are possibilities, not mandatory categories.

Use stable IDs such as N1, N2 and N3. Keep shared evidence, literature and obligation IDs in one register. Each branch must account for the same relevant results, including inconvenient evidence; prominence may differ, truth and provenance may not. Include a narrower framing when it is substantively useful. Do not offer an unsupported causal or novelty claim as an equally valid option. If only one framing is defensible, explain why and invite refinement rather than manufacture alternatives. Before results, keep endings conditional in every branch.

Give each branch a compact, comparable card:

- **Organizing question and contribution:** a working title, one-sentence claim, and what the reader would learn.
- **Literature route:** established knowledge → specific gap/tension → this contribution, with inspected source IDs and access limits.
- **Argument sketch:** a small labeled tree of the contribution, decisive claims, shared evidence IDs, assumptions and counterevidence.
- **Reader preview:** a short abstract spine and three to five section/figure beats, enough to judge the story without drafting a full paper.
- **Trade-offs:** what becomes central or supporting, important findings that must remain visible, strongest rival, added assumptions and unresolved evidence.
- **Readiness:** defensible with available evidence, provisional pending named evidence, or ruled out with reason. Keep ruled-out ideas outside the selectable set of supported framings.

Follow the cards with a concise comparison. Explain whether the branches are competing explanations, compatible emphases, or answer different questions. An evidence-based recommendation is allowed, but label it as a recommendation. Avoid numerical narrative scores or presenting a preferred branch as already selected.

## Author review and selection

After delivering the cards and comparison, ask the author to select an ID, request revisions, propose a coherent combination, or reject the set. Use an available input tool or a concise final question; keep the comparison visible in a durable artifact or the response. Set the narrative status to `awaiting author selection` and stop before expanding a selected full storyboard or handing off manuscript drafting. Do not treat silence, elapsed time, or a preselected UI option as a choice.

The user's explicit selection or delegation to choose is sufficient; do not ask again. Preserve a previously selected framing during a bounded revision unless the user reopens the choice or new evidence materially undermines it. A request to update this skill alone does not require asking the user to choose a research narrative now.

Record the selected branch ID/version, the user's instruction, requested adjustments, and unresolved evidence. Keep other branches as alternatives rather than deleting their scientific counterarguments. Selection chooses an editorial route; it cannot make unsupported claims true or change the status of a hypothesis. If the requested branch overstates the evidence, explain the conflict and offer a narrowed version for review.

## Expand the selected branch

Develop its full argument tree, claim records, literature comparisons and section/figure storyboard. Reuse the reviewed IDs and preserve the complete obligations register. If the author combines branches, establish one coherent central question and show how the secondary contribution supports it; do not concatenate incompatible claims or count shared evidence twice. Reopen selection only when a material change alters the reviewed contribution, not for routine wording or layout decisions.
